I had this dream about a workplace that i revisited, ive had a dream in this place before but time had moved on since i worked there. Its a psychological test of a place, basically the owners pay you to be there and put this massive puzzle together and see if anyone can find a way out. The working conditions are purposefully horrible, a big room with all of the pieces on the ground, a few towers scattered around it to watch the other people working, but most of the people working there are happy to because its a "game" and they want to play by the rules. There is an exit door and people can quit whenever, and its still a job so they go home at night. When you do something wrong or get in trouble they make you watch from the towers for your next shift, everyone in the towers or other "authority" positions are like that, everyone has the same rank. But the cycle continues. The goal of the experiment is to get everyone out of the building and free them from a hypothetical prison system, one of the ways to get out is to finish the puzzle. The puzzle is impossible though, its thousands and thousands of pieces wide and there isnt a workflow or order to it, on top of that last time i worked there i smuggled a piece out and threw it away in hopes to convince people it was pointless to continue but it didnt work, im sure other rebel type players had done similar things too before me so its not just one piece missing its possibly thousands There are a few other rooms that the prison has, one with a bunch of work cloths and tools, things to rent to do your job (rent meaning must be returned it didnt cost money) but it just pointed more and more that everything in the prison was self appointed. The rules other people made were made generations ago and everyone was following them just because, they weren't even _really_ a part of the game When i got there this time i returned with 5 other new players, and after the introduction i basically gave the explanation of my (cont.)

Previous experience with the place, one of them left because i "wasnt playing fairly" coming back, even though they let me and that wasnt against the rules to leave. There were many reasons to be here. Its interesting, you think you can win, you think its easy, but more importantly most need the money. The towering building was dense tan and grey, no color or promotions. No big logo on the outside to look friendly, just a tall building with no windows and blaring white lights.

It was open to workers 24/7, your schedule was decided by you and an interviewer when you first apply, but after that there is no changing it. Arriving 5 minutes off time either way gets you thrown immediately, or so they say, as long as the people inside decide to do so...

They were clear with the rules and why the building was made, and there wasnt anything hidden by the company. To most it actually looked like a helpful honest place, but I couldnt quite get my head around that. It all felt so wrong, and maybe it was supposed to?

Basically the whole operation was an experiment, and a psychological one at that. The goal was to get everyone to leave the building and quit the job. The building was built bare and rough and bland to make the environment unpleasant. The job itself was tedious and unfair. The directors of the experiment wanted to see how long a group would stay under such oppressed conditions.



Walking through the spinning doors I looked around to gather my environment. It already felt impressively soul sucking to enter. Everything was covered in a dull tan from both the old office style wallpaper and the yellow barely alive fluorescents that lit the place.

I expected the check in desk to have some sort of decoration, entrance flyers, scanners, anything to break it apart, but all that stood in the entrance was a small machine in the corner next to a clipboard, to scan a card and write a name and time.

The room had three doors, one was the spinning door i just came through, the one in front of me to the main floor of the job and one to the right. looking through the window it looked like rows of lockers.

Even though i had come in completely of my own free will, it almost felt like i was in cuffs, the place felt like a prison and i couldn't figure why anyone would want to stay here for longer than they needed to, though then again, i was here too.

Walking into the main room (and ignoring the lockers, as i hadnt brought anything with me) i stood at the entrance of the door overwhelmed by the place. The room was large and open, easily most of the building was this one room. People were scattered about, with no real pattern or groupings of workers, some of them talking, some of them alone.

Large dark grey pillars spotted the room, all connected by a balcony about 10 feet overhead. Some of the people were up there watching over the others on the ground. There were no tables, no chairs, or seats. There was nothing of comfort in the area. 

The biggest part of it all? puzzle pieces. a very very large puzzle. The job was to put together this massive puzzle easily thousands, if not tens of thousands, pieces. And looking at the environment that it was in made it sound all that more hopeless. 

The little dark pieces were scattered about all over the ground, no tables or mats to collect them. Some where piled in different places, some by color, some buy shape, some put together in impressive little patches. In my head i had confirmed my suspicions, the job itself was completely impossible. 

But.. i was here for the money, not the game so i stepped in and decided to ask a few questions. They only gave me two rules in orientation 1- the goal was to get everyone out and 2- completing the job would end the game. I thought surly they must have more that they arnt telling me

I stepped to a small group in the shade that seemed more interested in talking than in the puzzle, asking them what they thought of the job and a guide to what really happened inside the building.

At first they seemed hesitant to tell me, it seemed like they were trying to judge if i would agree with them on something. Eventually I got them to tell me what was happening. I was told that, for the most part, there were two known theories to "win" this game. The more popular theory was to complete the job. And to be fair, in the orientation we were told that if the job was finished then the operation would shut down. The other option was to get everyone to rebel and quit on their own, but this was lesser thought of.

The group i had run into were of the second mindset, and lucky them so was I. As someone who cant keep a job anyways there wasnt much a point in trying to be the best at something. Especially if that something had no reward.
